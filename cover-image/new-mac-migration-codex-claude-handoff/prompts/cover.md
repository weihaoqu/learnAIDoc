# Cover Prompt — New-Mac Migration

Generate a 16:9 conceptual flat-vector cover image illustrating a **new-machine migration of an AI agent stack** — copying a developer's configured Codex + Claude Code + cross-agent handoff workflow from an old Mac to a fresh one. The central visual idea is a **three-layered cube/stack being transferred between two laptops**, with the bottom two layers traveling and the top "Machine" layer left behind.

## Core composition

A clean editorial scene with three primary elements arranged left → center → right on the canvas:

- **Left**: A flat-vector laptop silhouette in deep ink blue, representing the **OLD Mac**. Inside the screen, a few abstract code lines and a small terminal cursor. Above or beside it, a small subtle "OLD" tag is unnecessary — symmetry alone communicates origin.
- **Center (visual anchor, slightly above the horizontal centerline)**: A **three-layered isometric stack of boxes**, mid-flight in motion between the two laptops. From bottom to top, the three layers are labeled (or schematically distinguished) as:
  - **Project** (bottom box, deep ink blue solid) — the largest, foundational
  - **User** (middle box, muted teal) — medium, the configuration personality layer
  - **Machine** (top box, pale gray/cool with a subtle dashed outline AND a small "X" or strike-through mark in soft red) — visibly NOT traveling with the stack
  - A small motion-line arc or curved arrow from the old laptop wraps **around or behind** the layered stack toward the new laptop, suggesting the bottom two layers are being carried over while the top "Machine" layer remains anchored to the old laptop with a thin dashed tether.
- **Right**: A second flat-vector laptop silhouette in the same deep ink blue, representing the **NEW Mac**. The screen displays a small clean checkmark icon or the abstract outlines of a fresh terminal prompt ready to receive the layers.

Connecting the three: one strong arcing arrow in cool teal that originates from the old laptop's screen, lifts the bottom two layers off the stack, and deposits them on the new laptop. The discarded top "Machine" layer hovers slightly above the old laptop, attached by a short dashed line, with a faint "✕" overlay to communicate "do not copy."

A faint horizontal **dashed migration baseline** beneath all three elements with a small clock or "90 min" tick mark hint near the right side, suggesting "across a 90-minute span." Very low-key.

## Palette (cool)

- **Background**: warm off-white or very pale cool gray (#F4F6FA-ish), large breathing room
- **Primary structural color**: deep ink blue (#1E3A5F) for the laptop outlines, layer-stack outlines, primary text
- **Secondary**: muted teal (#3E7B8F) for the migration arrow and the User layer
- **Tertiary**: pale slate (#9FB3C8) for the discarded Machine layer + dashed lines
- **Single warm accent**: very soft red/orange (#D96E58) used ONLY for the small ✕ mark on the Machine layer (signaling "don't copy")
- Optional pale yellow (#F5E6C8) as a single soft highlight on the new Mac's screen (the "fresh start" glow)
- Whitespace: 50%+ of the canvas. The scene should feel airy and architectural, not crowded.

## Rendering (flat-vector)

- Flat geometric shapes, no gradients except possibly one extremely subtle radial light behind the central layered stack
- Crisp 1.5–2px outlines, consistent stroke weight
- Schematic, almost diagrammatic — editorial illustration in the style of The Atlantic / Stripe Press / Anthropic's own marketing, not a SaaS hero
- The three stacked boxes should have a **slight isometric tilt** (axonometric, not full 3D perspective) to communicate stackability and modularity
- NO realistic humans, NO photographic textures, NO drop shadows, NO 3D extrusions beyond the slight isometric tilt
- The two laptops should be visually identical except for the small fresh-start glow on the right laptop's screen — symmetry communicates "same setup, different hardware"

## Text (title-only)

- **Title text on the canvas**: `New-Mac Migration` rendered in a clean modern sans-serif (DM Sans / Inter / Söhne / similar), in the deep ink blue, weight medium-bold
- Title positioned in the upper-left or upper-center, occupying no more than 30% of canvas width
- Title size large enough to be the second strongest visual element after the central layered stack
- **No subtitle**. **No body text. No tags. No code lines containing real prose.**

## Mood (balanced)

- Calm, considered, instructional — not urgent, not corporate-marketing-glossy
- Even visual weight between the old laptop, the layered stack, and the new laptop, with the stack being the slight visual anchor
- Conveys "this is a known-good procedure you can follow" rather than "shiny new product"

## Composition principles

- Whitespace: 40–60% breathing room around all elements
- Visual anchor: the central layered stack is the focal point; the two laptops are supporting structural elements
- Symmetry: the two laptops mirror each other; the migration arrow breaks the symmetry intentionally to communicate motion
- The title sits in the upper portion; the migration scene occupies the middle-to-lower 60% of the canvas
- No realistic humans, no photographic elements, no AI-generated faces of any kind
